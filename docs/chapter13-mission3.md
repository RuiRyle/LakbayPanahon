Six scenes introduce reason and rights, the French declaration of 1789, the Cadiz Constitution of 1812, a Philippine newspaper outlet in 1811, and evidence of circulation, receipt and response. The timeline explicitly moves from the 18th to the 19th century. Three clues, a decision and authored Explain help support each scene; five modern labelled diagrams are not presented as original historical documents.

Source boundaries:

- [National Park Service teaching material](https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/teachers/lessonplans/Thomas%20Jefferson%20Presentation.pdf): Enlightenment debates about reason and rights. The lesson does not portray fairness as invented by one writer or universally applied.
- [French presidency historical text](https://www.presidence-de-la-republique.fr/hist/10/ang/instit/text1.html): the 1789 declaration and its principles. Simplified explanations are paraphrases, not full translations. Adoption in France is not presented as automatic Philippine law or universal equality in practice.
- [Spanish Senate, Constitution of 1812](https://www.senado.es/web/conocersenado/senadohistoria/periodosconstitucionales/detalle/index.html?id=CONSTITUCION_DE_1812): divided governing tasks and the Philippine islands in Article 10. Territorial claims are not effective control everywhere; the text also had restrictions. It is not the current Philippine Constitution. The readable Senate text was consulted after direct retrieval of the Congress PDF failed.
- [NCCA, Philippine Literature in Spanish](https://ncca.gov.ph/about-ncca-3/subcommissions/subcommission-on-the-arts-sca/literary-arts/philippine-literature-in-spanish/): Del Superior Gobierno in 1811 and colonial regulation of circulation. No particular issue was examined; no specific foreign text is claimed to have appeared in it.
- The club booklet, delivery note and discussion minutes are explicitly fictional teaching examples. They explain evidence requirements without inventing historical readers, speeches or a single inevitable cause of later events.
