## Learning experience

Students help a fictional school museum build a connection exhibit. Six scenes
teach exports/imports through dated Iloilo examples, the Suez water link,
unequal resources for study, education and discussion, wider public concerns,
and a final selected-event timeline. Fictional family cards and classroom
conversations are explicitly distinguished from historical records.

The route activity asks for the direct water link available in a fictional
1870 exhibit task. Students must open the clues, choose the fitting route and
explain their choice. Selecting a route alone does not finish the scene.
The southern-Africa route is described as a different possible connection;
it is unsuitable for this particular task, not impossible or inherently unsafe.

Six labelled SVG diagrams use the established parchment and green palette.
The Suez sequence is symbolic, not a scaled map or a complete itinerary to the
Philippines. The text explains costs, agency and different opportunities.
It does not reduce national awareness to an automatic result of a canal or
assume learning originated only in Europe.

The five-topic assessment has ten medium main questions, ten distinct easy/hard
practice questions, mapped scene review, and existing five-question / three-correct
passing behavior. The configured first-pass reward is 50 XP and one mission
artifact. Local testing preview grades answers without saving these rewards.

## Sources and limits

- [NHCP Port of Iloilo registry](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/port-of-iloilo/): dated port and shipment evidence.
- [Suez Canal Authority history](https://www.suezcanal.gov.eg/English/About/SuezCanal/Pages/CanalHistory.aspx): canal opening.
- [Intramuros Museo ni Rizal](https://intramuros.gov.ph/mnr/): Madrid study example.
- [Intramuros economic-history exhibit](https://museum.intramuros.gov.ph/precolonial/manila-at-the-turn-of-the-century/): trade, resources and education context. The lesson does not repeat the exhibit's grouped port-opening date; it uses Iloilo's own marker.
