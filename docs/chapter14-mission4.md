# Chapter 14 Mission 4: Unity, Division, and Collective Identity

> Publication update - 28 October 2026: This release is now published to Firestore, backed up and field-verified. See [Chapters 7-14 publication](chapters7-14-publication.md). Older status sections below record the original authoring stage.

Locally authored and checked on 2026-10-28. Not published to Firestore or Hosting.

Six scenes distinguish cooperation on a shared aim, coordination, local belonging, disagreement and wider identity. Grade 5 definitions and explicitly fictional reading-group examples introduce the concepts without inventing historical meetings. Each scene provides three clues, a decision and authored Explain help. Five modern labelled diagrams are evidence guides, not reconstructed command routes or participant records.

Sources and boundaries:

- [UP Diliman research summary: They Are Not Foreigners](https://history.upd.edu.ph/publications/they-are-not-foreigners/): recognition of Chinese mestizos as legitimate members of particular Pampanga town communities despite legal distinctions. This is not every residents recorded opinion, equal political rights or proof of modern national identity. The abstract, not the complete archival dossier, was consulted. Historical source categories are not labels assigned to classmates.
- [NHCP registry: Agustin Sumuroy](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/agustin-sumuroy/): reported spread beyond Palapag. Reach is not a complete record of local coordination. Missing detail is also not proof that coordination never occurred. Graphic passages and sweeping first-revolt claims are excluded.
- [UCLA Ifugao research summary](https://anthro.ucla.edu/publication/indigenous-archaeology-in-the-philippines-decolonizing-ifugao-history/): examine community decisions within circumstances. The lesson does not invent a specific historical internal dispute or generalize one case to every community.
- Unity, division and identity are defined as analytical teaching concepts. Fictional cooperation and scheduling disagreements are not presented as historical facts. Shared concerns, joint action and collective identity need different evidence. No identity-based loyalty judgements, traitor labels, bravery rankings or ancestry disclosures are required.
