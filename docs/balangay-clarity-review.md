## Guiding question

What can boat evidence tell us about life and travel?

What can boat evidence tell us about life and travel? Follow Bayani from water routes to Butuan boat parts. Learn to explain a clue, recognise its limits, and care for heritage.

## What students should be able to explain

- Explain how waterways connect places and why travel plans depend on conditions.
- Use a boat feature to explain construction, distinguishing original evidence from a teaching model.
- Explain why a find and its recorded setting need specialist care.

## How can water connect communities?

How can people exchange goods when water lies between their homes?

The Philippines is an archipelago: a group of islands. Boats could link communities by carrying people, goods and messages. Rivers could connect inland places with the coast.

Think of a route as a connection, not a guarantee. A boat that suits a river may not suit rough open water. Travellers need to consider each part of a journey.

Open the clues. Explain how a waterway could connect two places.

**Try this example:** An inland community follows a river to exchange products at the coast.

**Explain your thinking:** Which choice explains how people or goods could move between the two places?

## A boat part is a clue

What can an old boat part tell us?

Researchers study wooden boat remains from Butuan in northeastern Mindanao, often called balangay boats. The surviving wood and joins provide clues about boat construction. A join is where parts meet.

An original plank is evidence from the past. A new classroom model or diagram explains selected features. It is not the original boat.

Look at the labelled teaching image. Identify the evidence, then say what it supports. A plank does not name every passenger or record every voyage.

**Try this example:** An old plank can show a wooden join; a new diagram helps explain that join.

**Explain your thinking:** Which object comes from the past, and which was made to explain it?

## Two joins, two different jobs

How did builders connect wooden parts?

Follow diagram A first. A plank is a shaped piece of wood. A dowel is a wooden pin. Here, it joins two plank edges.

Now follow diagram B. A frame supports the inside of the boat. A lug is a raised feature on the inside of a plank. Bindings tie the frame to the lug.

Do not mix up the two jobs: joining plank edges and attaching an internal support. These simplified details show one example; recovered boats differ.

**Try this example:** A uses a wooden pin between plank edges. B uses bindings to secure an internal frame.

**Explain your thinking:** Find the two separate details: plank edges with a pin, and a frame tied to a raised lug.

## A plan depends on today's clues

Why might a crew change a familiar route?

A boat journey depends on the boat, route, water and weather. A current is moving water. Conditions can change, even on a route people know.

In our fictional task, a small boat does not suit rough open water. Compare that clue with each route description. A shorter route is not automatically a suitable route.

Explain a choice using the conditions given. The task is a teaching example, not a record of an ancient voyage or advice for a real trip.

**Try this example:** If water becomes rougher, a crew may need to wait or change its plan.

**Explain your thinking:** Compare what is known about the boat with the conditions described for today.

## Keep the clue and its setting

Why does a find's location matter?

Context means where a find was located and how it lay beside other finds. Researchers record these relationships before disturbance. Specialist heritage care is called conservation.

Old wood can be fragile. Moving or cleaning it without guidance can damage the object and its context. Leave possible remains in place and tell responsible site staff. Do not dig or test them yourself.

Your report: describe a feature, explain what it supports, and state what remains unknown. A photograph does not replace the full context.

**Try this example:** Two nearby wooden pieces need recording and examination before anyone assumes how they fit.

**Explain your thinking:** Which action protects both the object and information about where it was found?

## Final recap

What did you discover? Boats and waterways could connect communities. Surviving wooden parts help researchers study construction. Why does it matter? These clues help us investigate people's skills and connections. How do we know? We examine remains and their recorded setting. Models explain selected details; they do not record every voyage or passenger. Protect finds and their context.

## Example of improved feedback

Diagram B shows a frame tied to a raised lug inside a plank. Which explanation fits?

- **A: The lug is the whole boat.**
  Feedback: A lug is a raised feature on a plank, not the entire boat.

- **B: The binding attaches the internal support to the lug.**
  Feedback: The frame is a support. Bindings secure it to a raised lug in this example.

- **C: The binding is a wooden pin between plank edges.**
  Feedback: That describes the dowel detail, not this binding.

- **D: The drawing proves every boat was built identically.**
  Feedback: The drawing shows selected details, not a rule for all boats.
