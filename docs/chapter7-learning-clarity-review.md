How did central colonial government work, and what can its records tell us?

### An office under the Spanish Crown

The governor-general held a leading office in Spanish colonial government. The Spanish Crown means the monarchy and its authority, not just a royal headpiece.

An office and its holder are different: several people can hold the same position at different times. Responsibilities and government arrangements also changed over the long colonial period.

Try a fictional museum label: replace “elected president of an independent republic” with the correct colonial relationship. This does not mean the official could ignore laws or other institutions. The diagram is a simplified guide, not a chart for every year.

**Key idea:** The governor-general was a leading colonial official acting under the Spanish Crown, not an elected president of an independent republic.

**Key idea:** The office had civil and military responsibilities; people, resources and actions determined how duties were performed.
