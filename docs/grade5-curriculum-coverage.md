# Grade 5 curriculum coverage review

Reviewed 28 October 2026; status wording refreshed 30 October 2026 against the current editorial review JSON and recorded publication notes. MATATAG Grade 5 is confirmed by the user. **The four-era structure can stay, but the prologue and fifteen chapters must not be described as complete Grade 5 curriculum coverage.**

## Basis and limits

This provisional review compares authored content with the Grade 5 section of
the DepEd-authored August 2023 MATATAG Araling Panlipunan guide, printed pages
22–30. Its [official PDF URL](https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/1-FINAL-MATATAG-Araling-Panlipunan-CG-2023-Grades-4-10.pdf)
returned HTTP 403; the text was inspected in a [publicly hosted copy](https://eduksama.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/FINAL-MATATAG-Araling-Panlipunan-CG-2023-Grades-4-10.pdf).
School-specific adoption, amendments and pacing are not confirmed.

The [older DepEd Grade 5 portal entry](https://lrmds.deped.gov.ph/detail/15672)
has a different historical endpoint. Mixing that older scope with MATATAG can
hide later nationalism gaps. An older [nationalism resource](https://lrmds.deped.gov.ph/detail/25734)
is tagged Grade 6; that tag should not override the Grade 5 section of the
guide being compared here.

Evidence uses the dated 27 October curriculum snapshot plus selected local
early-chapter enrichment, and Chapters 5–15 authoring plans. No fresh Firestore
curriculum reads or writes were made for this audit. Early local revisions may
not all match the current live revision. Source mentions and document counts
are locators, not proof of adequate teaching, historical accuracy or mastery.

The 30 October refresh reconciles this document with
`data/grade5-coverage-review.json`; it does not reclassify competencies from
keyword matches or establish current live Firestore state. Chapter 15's five
missions and final challenge have recorded publication/readback evidence from
28 October. Publication is distinct from independent teaching and accuracy approval.

## Highest-priority changes

### 1. Review the final era's published teaching and remaining depth gaps

Chapter 15 now supplies the connected sequence proposed in the original review:
trade, travel and access in Mission 1; public contributions in Mission 2; parish
reform and Pedro Pelaez in Mission 3; the Cavite accounts and GOMBURZA in Mission 4;
and reform goals, La Solidaridad and La Liga Filipina in Mission 5. All five
missions and the final evidence challenge have recorded publication on 28 October.
See the [final mission notes](chapter15-mission5.md) and
[chapter challenge record](chapter15-challenge.md).

Q4.4–Q4.7 are provisionally mapped in the current editorial review. Q4.1–Q4.3
remain partial: nationalism, the wider liberal-ideas sequence and emerging
middle-class contributions still need deeper review. The named examples and
interactive tasks locate relevant teaching; they do not establish that the full
competency is adequately taught or understood. De la Torre remains an example to
check against the intended scope rather than a reason to describe the entire
reform movement as absent.

Keep the early-resistance missions as foundations and review the existing final
sequence before adding more required work. Changes to mission or challenge
requirements must account for students' saved completions and earned badges.

### 2. Turn evidence caution into usable historical knowledge

Many later questions reward choosing the best-qualified statement. Students
also need concrete dates, places, people, actions and consequences. For each
topic, first teach a named example, then ask students to use it. Keep uncertainty
where warranted; avoid making every distractor an obvious universal claim.

Use a timeline investigation, a route-and-message task, or a short newspaper
comparison. Reward explaining a connection with evidence, not remembering
isolated dates or earning points for violence.

### 3. Fill earlier depth gaps

- Origins: compare sourced knowledge traditions with scientific explanations
  respectfully; never present an invented tale as a community's authentic account.
- Geography: verify explicit upstream/downstream community relationships, not
  only compass directions and island groups.
- Early community life: develop women's contributions through more than generic roles.
- Cultural life: directly compare objects and practices; check architecture and
  health as teaching targets rather than assume a broad chapter title covers them.
- Islam: connect religious teaching and governing roles without treating all
  Muslim communities as identical or asking students to practise a faith.

The [Chapter 2 family knowledge draft](chapter2-family-learning-review.md) adds a
named, source-limited Ifugao example and a stronger kinship lesson-to-practice
sequence. It is local and unpublished. Q1.7 remains partial because this example
does not establish the range of women's contributions in early Philippine
communities or their dates.

A separate [Mission 2 dated-role draft](chapter2-women-role-learning.md) now adds
Plasencia's 1589 Tagalog account to teaching and both spiritual-leadership quiz
variants. It is unpublished. The account was written during Spanish rule and
supplies one reported cultural role, so Q1.7 remains partial.

### 4. Assess something the student creates

Report frames currently suggest explanations, but many interactions still end
with choosing an answer. Add an age-appropriate product: an evidence board,
short narrated explanation, or public-interest poster. A saved evidence card
alone does not prove the student can explain it. AI can offer prompts and word
help; it should not author the student's entire explanation or determine
historical truth without the provided sources.

The review should check content, explanation and a supported connection. Keep
the task short, accessible and private. Do not automatically add this to era
unlock requirements until the assessment behavior and saved-progress impact
have been designed.

## Recommendation

Keep four navigation eras. They are not four quarterly containers: the first
era covers much of the first two quarters, while the colonial chapters span
the third and parts of the fourth. Use the user-confirmed MATATAG baseline,
review the published final-era sequence and strengthen the identified
partial targets. Verify topic → teaching scene → activity → assessment before
claiming coverage. Independent teacher review is still required for approval.
