## chapter5-mission3

How can we explain Mactan using causes, conditions and evidence?

### Who could give orders?

Mactan is near Cebu. Lapulapu was a leader there. The NHCP marker records his refusal to recognize the king of Spain and the position claimed for Rajah Humabon over Cebu and Mactan.

Authority means the power to give orders and expect obedience. Magellan sought recognition of authority, but asking for recognition is different from receiving agreement.

Zula, another leader in Mactan, sought Magellan’s support against Lapulapu. Compare their choices: refusal and a request for outside support. This difference matters; one leader’s response cannot stand for everyone or for a modern nation-state.

**Key idea:** Lapulapu refused the demanded authority. Zula sought Magellan’s support. Local leaders did not all make the same choice.

- **What issue was central to Lapulapu’s refusal?** The authority demanded by Magellan. The dispute concerned whose rule or position would be recognized.
- **A classroom comparison puts Lapulapu’s refusal beside Zula’s request for support. What does it show?** Local leaders made different choices. The two responses should not be merged into one opinion for everyone.
- **What does authority mean here?** Power to give orders and expect obedience. The lesson examines who was being asked to recognize whose authority.
- **A report says Magellan’s demand proves local agreement. What should be checked?** The actual local responses to the demand. A demand identifies what someone wants, not what others accepted.
- **Which leader is associated with refusing the demanded recognition?** Lapulapu. The NHCP marker connects his refusal with the dispute over authority.
- **Why should a report name Zula as well as Lapulapu?** To show differing local decisions within the conflict. Naming distinct choices avoids treating all residents as one voice.

### Build the cause chain

A cause helps explain why something happened. For Mactan, begin with decisions about authority rather than only remembering a date.

Magellan became involved in local disputes and attempted to enforce demands for obedience. The NHCP marker records his order to burn Buaya after its refusal to recognize authority. Coercion means trying to make people obey through force or threats.

Build this cause chain: demand for recognition, refusal, then a decision to use force. Explaining the chain does not make an attack acceptable or unavoidable. In a fictional report task, replace “everyone agreed after exchanging gifts” with the evidence about demands and different responses.

**Key idea:** Demands met refusal, and Magellan tried to impose authority by force. Explaining this decision does not justify the harm.

<details>

- **Which explanation connects the dispute with the conflict?** Demands met refusal, and Magellan attempted to enforce authority by force. The chain links decisions rather than assuming agreement.
- **A fictional report says gifts made everyone agree to foreign rule. What evidence should replace that claim?** The demands, refusal and decision to use force. Gift exchange cannot erase the recorded disagreement about authority.
- **What is coercion?** Trying to make people obey through force or threats. The word identifies a method of imposing demands.
- **A report says refusal made an attack inevitable. What distinction is needed?** Refusal and the decision to attack were separate actions. Explaining a conflict must still recognize choices made by participants.
- **What does a cause help explain?** Why something happened. A cause connects relevant actions and conditions rather than naming only the event.
- **How can a learner explain the burning recorded at Buaya responsibly?** Identify the use of force and its harm without praising it. Historical explanation can name coercion while recognizing its effects on people.

### Read the shoreline clue

A shoreline can affect how boats reach land. The lesson’s account-based example describes low tide and coral preventing boats from approaching the beach closely. Magellan’s landing party crossed shallow water.

Follow the teaching diagram from boats to shallow water to beach. Connect a feature with an effect: boats farther away could not take the landing party directly to the shore.

This is a conceptual diagram, not an exact battle map. It does not show every position, distance or movement. Conditions mattered together with decisions and the defenders’ actions; one shoreline feature cannot explain every part of a battle.

**Key idea:** Shallow water and coral limited the boats’ approach. Conditions affected movement alongside decisions and the defenders’ actions.

- **What kind of image is the lesson’s shoreline diagram?** A simplified teaching model of conditions and movement. It explains a relationship without claiming exact battle positions.

### Result, then what happened later?

The Battle of Mactan took place on 27 April 1521. Mactan’s defenders defeated the attacking force led by Magellan. Magellan died in the battle. The defenders’ collective action mattered.

Sort the sequence: demands and refusal came before the battle; the attacking party’s defeat was an immediate result; surviving members continued the voyage afterward. The death of a leader did not mean everyone died.

Mactan is remembered as resistance to an attempt to impose authority. It did not create a modern Philippine republic that day or end all later expeditions. We study the result respectfully; a learning reward does not celebrate injury or death.

**Key idea:** Mactan’s defenders won on 27 April 1521 and Magellan died. Surviving expedition members continued afterward.

- **Why distinguish Mactan’s victory from modern national independence?** They concern different events and historical settings. Resistance in 1521 did not establish a modern republic or end later colonial attempts.

### Evidence or artistic retelling?

Antonio Pigafetta participated in the expedition and witnessed the battle. His account is valuable but records a participant’s viewpoint. The NQC notes that he did not identify Lapulapu among the combatants.

This does not establish a personal duel, and it does not prove Lapulapu was absent. Lapulapu is remembered for leadership in resistance; the defenders’ actions must also be recognized.

Try a fictional exhibit task: caption a dramatic picture as an artistic retelling. A modern monument is not an exact portrait made in 1521. Remembering a leader does not require inventing his exact age, appearance or speech. Ask which details come from evidence and which are artistic choices.

**Key idea:** A retelling can remember resistance without proving a personal duel, exact appearance or speech. Identify what the evidence supports.

## Explain Mactan with three connections

Connect the demand for authority, local refusals and the decision to use force. Explain how shallow water and coral affected the landing party, alongside the defenders’ actions. Then state the outcome on 27 April 1521.

Try this fictional report frame: The conflict involved [demand and response]. A condition affected [movement]. The result was [outcome]. The evidence does not establish [uncertain detail]. Keep artistic retellings separate from recorded evidence.
