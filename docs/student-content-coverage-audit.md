# Student learning-content coverage audit

Audit date: 2026-10-27T13:06:11.538Z. Read-only snapshot of the original Firestore curriculum; no learner data was read or modified.

## Finding

The current build implements the project roadmap's Era 1 prologue and Chapters 1–4, but it is not a complete Grade 5 Philippine-history course. Chapters 5–14 in Eras 2–4 are not present in this snapshot. Even Era 1 needs instructional expansion: having a topic tag, a scene and a question is not enough evidence of understanding.

There are 17 active missions, 90 scenes, 170 quiz-bank questions and 188 practice questions. Of 89 distinct mission/topic pairs, 0 lack a scene mapping, 4 lack quiz questions and 0 lack practice. This establishes navigation coverage only. The five-topic blueprints were authoring choices, not competency validation. Each main quiz still selects five questions from its bank.

60 configured local scene images are missing from the workspace at audit time. Artwork must teach a relevant detail; attractive backgrounds alone cannot replace a map, labelled diagram or evidence card.

In 60 scenes every exploration discovery is an exact excerpt already in the scene body. Opening these clues is interaction, but does not add a new observation or test an interpretation. Many new distractors make implausible absolute claims, letting students eliminate answers without understanding the history. These are editorial findings, not measured item-difficulty results.

## Topics outside the main quiz blueprint

Four mapped practice/review topics have no main-quiz question carrying the same topic ID:

- chapter2-mission2 / respect-for-traditions (scenes: chap2-mission2-scene5, chap2-mission2-scene6)
- chapter2-mission2 / ancestor-veneration (scenes: chap2-mission2-scene1, chap2-mission2-scene2)
- chapter2-mission2 / cultural-diversity (scenes: chap2-mission2-scene5)
- chapter2-mission3 / respect-for-religion (scenes: chap2-mission3-scene3, chap2-mission3-scene6)

These are additional practice topics, not proof of four missing quiz questions: the configured five-slot quiz blueprints may assess related ideas under another tag. Reconcile topic meanings before adding questions or changing passing rules. Otherwise some practice can appear unrelated to the diagnosis. Topic IDs need semantic review, not automatic merging.

## Roadmap and curriculum boundary

The comparison uses the user's Final Grade 5 Learning Roadmap v1.1 attachment: four prologue missions, thirteen missions in Chapters 1–4, and ten future chapters. All seventeen planned Era 1 mission titles have counterparts in the live catalog. Future chapters are absent, not partially completed.

The [published DepEd Grade 5 reference](https://lrmds.deped.gov.ph/detail/15672) explicitly includes geography/climate and island formation alongside early societies and colonial history. The current geography mission has no explicit tropical-climate objective; formation explains shoreline change without explicitly teaching continental shelf. These need an alignment decision and sourced teaching. This reference is a legacy K–12 portal listing, not certification of alignment to the currently applicable curriculum. The project's teacher must confirm the intended curriculum version and competency codes before an approval claim.

## Editorial review by mission

### The Philippines as an Archipelago: Location and Geography — P1

Scene IDs: prologue-mission1-scene1, prologue-mission1-scene2, prologue-mission1-scene3, prologue-mission1-scene4, prologue-mission1-scene5.

Currently taught: Archipelago, directions, island groups, surroundings and map legends are introduced.

Gap / change: Add a labelled Philippines-in-Southeast-Asia map, a compass task locating real places, and an island-group location task. The lesson says to use a map but currently supplies no actual map. Tropical location and climate are absent as explicit teaching targets.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Locate a place and explain one geographical relationship, rather than only identify the word archipelago.

### Formation of the Philippine Islands: Scientific Explanations — P1

Scene IDs: prologue-mission2-scene1, prologue-mission2-scene2, prologue-mission2-scene3, prologue-mission2-scene4, prologue-mission2-scene5.

Currently taught: Moving plates, volcanic land-building, shoreline change and evidence types are introduced.

Gap / change: Add a step-by-step labelled plate/volcano diagram and a shallow-shelf versus deep-channel model; explicitly explain continental shelf in child-friendly words. Distinguish process explanations from the misleading claim that all islands were one land bridge.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Interpret a diagram and explain why a shoreline changes; pair scientific evidence with an accurately attributed traditional story only after source review.

### Archaeological Evidence of Early Human Presence — P1

Scene IDs: prologue-mission3-scene1, prologue-mission3-scene2, prologue-mission3-scene3, prologue-mission3-scene4, prologue-mission3-scene5.

Currently taught: Tabon, Callao, archaeological context, dating uncertainty and respectful care are named.

Gap / change: Add a location map and sourced evidence cards comparing the two sites, find types and what each supports. Explain fossil, species and dating with a worked example. Chronological context is too abstract without a timeline.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Compare two evidence cards and justify a limited conclusion; do not add unsupported first-arrival dates.

### Early Population Movements and the Peopling of the Philippines — P1

Scene IDs: prologue-mission4-scene1, prologue-mission4-scene2, prologue-mission4-scene3, prologue-mission4-scene4, prologue-mission4-scene5.

Currently taught: Multiple journeys, Austronesian languages, Batanes and limits of evidence are introduced.

Gap / change: Add a labelled movement-model map, a worked language/object evidence comparison and a scaffolded explanation of more than one population-history model. Current repeated cautions do not show enough of how the explanations work.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Choose a claim using two supplied sources; distinguish language relationship from biological ancestry.

### Life in the Barangay — P2

Scene IDs: mission1-scene3, mission1-scene5, mission1-scene1, mission1-scene2, mission1-scene4, mission1-scene6.

Currently taught: Community organization, leadership, unequal social positions, customs and cooperation are explained with scenarios.

Gap / change: Retain the richer scenarios. Add a dated/contextualized comparison example and link its recap forward to Chapter 2 so repeated leadership lessons deepen rather than restart.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Connect leadership, duties and unequal freedom in one explained situation.

### Trade and Livelihood — P1

Scene IDs: mission2-scene1, mission2-scene2, mission2-scene6, mission2-scene5, mission2-scene3, mission2-scene4.

Currently taught: Farming, fishing, craft and exchange appear; a concrete barter investigation is present.

Gap / change: Expand the very short opening livelihood scenes with a worked resource-to-skill-to-product example. Remove the stray word Copy from mission2-scene5.decisionQuestion. The simulated exchange should remain labelled fictional.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Explain how two different livelihoods meet needs before planning an exchange.

### Roles and Responsibilities in Early Filipino Communities — P2

Scene IDs: chap2-mission1-scene1, chap2-mission1-scene2, chap2-mission1-scene3, chap2-mission1-scene4, chap2-mission1-scene5.

Gap / change: Explain the named social terms using a carefully sourced comparison table; naming two sets of terms alone does not teach their meanings. Avoid implying one system covered all communities.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Compare a specified pair of experiences using rights and duties, not only select a respectful statement.

Evidence students should demonstrate: Build a short claim with two evidence examples and one present-day connection.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Expand Prologue Mission 1 first: an actual labelled map, guided example, learner location task, then a recap. Decide where tropical location/climate belongs. Keep the existing mission ID and completion records.
2. Expand formation and archaeology with labelled diagrams, site cards and chronological context; then scaffold population-history explanations with two supplied sources.
3. Add sourced concrete examples to Asian trade, goods, ports, writing, crafts and regional diversity. Reuse examples in the heritage synthesis so learning builds across missions.
4. Revise questions after teaching is expanded. Keep the agreed five-question, three-correct mission rule, but assess the new objectives; extra questions in the bank do not create missing teaching. Use plausible misconceptions and worked feedback. Topic-matched retries are not empirically proven equivalent difficulty.
5. Review the complete content with the teacher and actual Grade 5 learners before describing it as educationally validated. Add scene counts only when the objectives require them; avoid another fixed scene quota.

Do not make AI responsible for missing core instruction or allow it to supply assessed facts that are absent from authored lessons. Authored explanation, hints and review must work without an AI response. No AI integration changes are part of this audit.
