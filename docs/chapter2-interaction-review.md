## Learning findings

- **Social comparison scenes:** the diagram adds a structured rights/duties comparison; fictional cases ask students to compare restrictions. This supplies useful reasoning beyond opening decorative clues. Some incorrect choices use obvious absolute claims, so choosing correctly is not strong evidence of mastery by itself.
- **Beliefs scenes:** ritual-purpose clues provide context beyond the brief scene body. Later oral-tradition and artifact clues mainly guide source interpretation. These can be useful reading scaffolds, but should not be described as finding new physical evidence in an illustration.
- **Islam/exchange scenes:** the locator, timeline and contribution cards have different instructional roles: where, when and how. They should not be presented as records of actual voyages or as proof that all people in a location shared a belief. The candidate text explicitly states these limits.
- **Vocabulary:** the revised comparison scenes have multi-term help lists. Earlier narrative scenes often have only a single key term. Existing Explain/help content should be included in comprehension observation before deciding more definitions are needed.
- **Illustrations inspected:** the family scene visibly depicts an older person speaking with children; the harvest scene depicts food and a community gathering. These support the activity context. Neither image establishes the accuracy of particular clothing, ornaments, architecture or ceremonial details. Avoid assessing such details as historical facts without source review.
